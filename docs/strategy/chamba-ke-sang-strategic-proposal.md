## 1. Project intelligence summary

Chamba ke sang has three real assets: named people with documented skills, a community already organised in Mystic Village, and a valley that day-trippers pass through without stopping. Its binding constraints are room capacity, unfinished infrastructure in Saal Valley, and almost no verified commercial data.

**Documents reviewed.** 11 project files, plus 2 uploads in this chat.

**What we know.** Stated as fact in the documents:

- NotOnMap (Unknown Destinations Pvt Ltd, Chaminoo, Barour) has been a knowledge partner to Himalay Unnati Mission (SSRDP/Art of Living) for 3 years. *Survey Report, §III.*
- The government-linked mandate covers 45 panchayats of Chamba block. Cluster 1 is a 13-panchayat pilot with the District Development Officer, targeting 700 people trained and 200+ direct beneficiaries. *Inception Report, §1–3, pp.5–8.*
- Mystic Village Khajjiar is the hub: about 2,200 m, 87 households, on the ridge where Kohlari, Singi and Khajjiar panchayats meet. Homestays are "currently limited to five households". *Inception Report, §6, p.11–12.*
- Auda and Talai are the spokes. Auda sits at \~1,600 m, 6–7 km from Khajjiar, has an active Yuvak Mandal of 30–40 people, and a few homestays plus nascent camping. *Auda Report, pp.4, 10.*
- In Saal Valley, 17 properties are profiled with room counts. Most are "not registered yet / under construction", and Surendar Ji's house has no washroom. *Resource Mapping, pp.1–48; Saal valley.xlsx.*
- Mystic Village has three organised community groups (Team Parivartan plastic-free initiative, Team Udaan women's SHG, Village Development Committee) and 57 named people with listed skills. *People's Profile, pp.2–4.*

**What the documents suggest.** These are observations or claims, not verified facts:

- Dalhousie hotels capture the tourist flow and send guests to Chamba on same-day trips, with 30–40 cars a day in peak season. *Homestay Study, §2.5–2.6, pp.13–16.* Undated student fieldwork, so treat it as a hypothesis.
- Khajjiar is mostly a day-visit destination, and homestays act as hotel overflow. *Homestay Study, Anjali Homestay field visit.*
- Privacy, security and hygiene are the main deterrents to homestays. *Homestay Study, consumer survey.* The sample was 48 respondents aged 22–27, so it is not representative.
- Mystic Village community readiness is "high". *Inception Report, comparative table, p.21.* This is NotOnMap's own assessment.
- Auda's 550-year-old Kruin tree, the king's-meat legend and the shepherd-route heritage come from oral history. *Auda Report, pp.5–10.*

**What we do not know.** Each of these changes the design:

- Rooms, beds, prices and occupancy for any live Mystic Village homestay
- Current traveller volumes and length of stay in Khajjiar, Chamba town or Dalhousie
- What hosts earn now, and what they would consider fair pay for a session
- Who books today (direct, OTA, operators) and through which payment rails
- Which experiences hosts actually want to offer, and which they would refuse
- Whether H2O House exists as a bookable base, and who owns it (the Journey Builder uses it as "your base")

**What needs field validation before Phase 2 commits to it.** Room capacity per house; each host's willingness to run sessions and how often; a price floor per session from hosts themselves; road and travel times between Chaminoo, Mystic Village, Auda and Chamba town; winter access; and consent for every named person.

**Assets and advantages.**

- People with named, specific skills: Chamba Chukh, Siddu and Kaku Di Rasoi cooks, Gaddi dress, pine-needle and chakla craft, folk singers, paragliding and mountaineering guides, 4x4 drivers.
- Organised community institutions in Mystic Village: the SHG, a youth waste team and the Village Development Committee.
- Landscape layers within 1–2 hours of each other: the Khajjiar meadow and ridge, Saal river valley, Auda viewpoint, and the Chamba town heritage core.
- Seasonal culture: the Manimahesh Yatra rituals, Nawala, jatras and fairs. *Auda Report; Homestay Study, "Devotional Tourism".*
- Documented local knowledge that can be packaged as learning: about 40 medicinal plants on the H2O Trail, beekeeping and wall-hives, and shepherd routes.

**Major constraints.**

- Small, uneven room supply. Groups larger than about 12 need a campsite.
- Infrastructure gaps: washrooms, internet, roads, water and power. *Inception Report, §15, pp.27–28.*
- Winter snow and access limits from December to February.
- Forest NOCs are needed for trails. *Inception Report, §15.*
- Thin hospitality training to date.
- NotOnMap's own coordination bandwidth (task sheet: hiring for admin and a project manager is open).

**Opportunities.**

1. Convert day-trippers into overnight guests.
2. Learning-based stays (craft, food, plants) for longer trips.
3. Institutional and educational groups in the off-peak season.
4. A transparent benefit record. No peer in the region offers one, and it fits NotOnMap's development mandate.

**Key strategic questions for the phases ahead.**

1. Is a seven-day product justified, or is the real unit a 3–4 day core with optional extensions?
2. Should NotOnMap be the merchant of record (it takes payment) or a facilitator (hosts are paid directly)?
3. Which one experience is so specific to Chamba that someone would travel for it alone?
4. How many guests a month can Mystic Village absorb without overloading a few families?

**What this means for Chamba ke sang.**

- **No benchmark measures money reaching households.** The best one (Ripple Score) measures spend with local *businesses* inside the destination. A Chamba record that takes the full traveller price as its denominator and separates household-level payments from business revenue would be a real step beyond the field. It is also harder, which Phase 3 must design for.
- **The durable Himalayan models share three features.** There is a fixed share to a village fund (10–20%), community or individual ownership of the asset, and a cap or rotation that spreads guests. All three are absent from the current documents.
- **Seven nights is proven only for older, international, walking-oriented guests.** For urban Indian travellers the evidence points to shorter, single-base stays (section 4). The seven-day journey should be one variant, not the core unit.

**Segment hypotheses for Phase 2 to test.** These are not findings.

1. **The weekend-plus urban Indian** (Delhi NCR, Chandigarh, Punjab): 3–4 nights, one base, wants calm plus one or two "only here" experiences. This is the largest pool and is reached through the B2B operators in the strategy doc.
2. **The learning traveller**, Indian or international (craft, food, plants): 5–7 nights, values the named hosts, and is the best fit for the benefit record.
3. **The international slow walker**: 7+ nights, walking between villages; mirrors Village Ways. Small numbers, high value per stay.
4. **Families**: need private bathrooms, short transfers and child-safe activities. Readiness is the gate.

**What we could not establish.** Willingness to pay in rupees for a Chamba village night or session; Reddit and forum sentiment specific to Chamba homestays (not searched this phase); international arrivals in Chamba district.

## 5. Frameworks worth applying

Use seven tools, each tied to one decision, and drop the rest. The most important input is not a framework but a law. The Homestay Study's 2008 scheme is outdated, so every host on the product must meet the 2025 rules.

**Regulatory baseline: Himachal Pradesh Home Stay Rules, 2025.** These were notified on 25 June 2025 and [replace the 2008 scheme](https://www.taxaj.com/learn/?p=90734); units registered under the old scheme must register afresh.

- **Categories by tariff:** Silver up to ₹3,000 per room per night, Gold ₹3,000–10,000, Diamond above ₹10,000.
- **Annual fees:** In a Gram Panchayat area, Silver costs ₹3,000 a year.
- **Required equipment:** Secondary sources report that [fire extinguishers, CCTV and a water filter](https://raachotrekkers.com/himachal-homestay-rules-2025/) are mandatory.
- **Concessions:** Women owners get an [extra 5% fee concession](https://himachalheadlines.com/?p=54016).

Chamba operators have [protested the fees](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/homestay-operators-in-chamba-protest-state-new-policy-over-high-fees/amp). Implications: registration status becomes readiness gate #1. Fees are a real cost the price must cover, and the CCTV rule needs a guest-privacy design. Phase 3 must read the gazette itself, not summaries.

| Framework | Decision it informs | Where it lands |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [GSTC Industry Criteria](https://gstcouncil.org/gstc-criteria/gstc-destination-criteria) (tour operator and accommodation) | What "ready" means and which claims NotOnMap can make | Readiness tiers, anti-greenwashing policy |
| Leakage accounting (Ripple Score method, extended to the full price) | How the benefit record's numerator and denominator are defined | Transparency system |
| Theory of change (output → outcome → impact) | Which claims are verified, which are estimates, which are hopes | Traveller statement wording, KPIs |
| Limits of acceptable change / carrying capacity, set with residents | Guests per house per month; rotation rules; what stays off-limits | Governance, pilot caps |
| Service blueprint | Where a multi-host day breaks: handoffs, transport, payment, emergencies | Operating model |
| Jobs to be done | What each segment is "hiring" the trip for: rest, learning, meaning, a story to tell | Segments, journey variants |
| Participatory rural appraisal: seasonal calendar, resource mapping, ranking | Which experiences the community offers, and when | Consultation (NotOnMap already uses these: Survey Report §XIV–XV) |

**Deliberately not used now.** SROI needs a baseline and budget the pilot lacks; revisit after one season of data. KANO needs survey data we don't have. Doughnut Economics and Experience Economy are descriptive here and would not change a decision. The Value Proposition Canvas is folded into jobs-to-be-done.

Families, small friend groups and the international slow walker become variants of these two, not separate products.

## 7. Destination positioning

Position Chamba ke sang on people and what they know, not on landscape. Chamba is an old hill kingdom whose court crafts still carry Geographical Indication status, sitting within an hour of living Gaddi and Gujjar pastoral cultures. Use "the Chamba the day-trip skips" as the marketing hook, and the verified benefit record as proof, not as the headline.

**Evidence that Chamba has something specific:**

- **Crafts with GI status:** Chamba Rumal (No. 79, [registered 2007](https://himcoste.hp.gov.in/Patent%20Information%20Centre/Pdf/gi%20brochure1.pdf)), [Chamba chappal](https://search.ipindia.gov.in/GIRPublicSearch/Application/Details/614), and [Chamba metal art (2026)](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/eight-more-traditional-products-himachal-get-gi-tag-796.htm). The state is also pursuing GI status for Chamba chukh.
- **A living festival with a strong story:** the [International Minjar Fair](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/chambas-minjar-fair-continues-to-uphold-spirit-of-communal-unity-643433) runs for about a week in late July–early August. It opens with a Muslim family offering the minjar at the Laxmi Narayan temple, a role the family has held for generations.
- **People:** 57 named people in Mystic Village with documented skills (Chamba Chukh, Gaddi dress, pine-needle craft, folk song, paragliding), plus the Gujjar village at Sel (on hold).
- **Knowledge:** about 40 plants with local medicinal uses on the H2O Trail notebook.

**Brand essentials for Territory C:**

- **Promise:** You'll learn something real from the person whose life it is, and you'll see what your stay paid them.
- **Tone:** plain and specific. Use names and skills, never pity or "untouched". Hosts are teachers and professionals, not subjects.
- **For international guests**, the name needs a line: *Chamba ke sang — in the company of Chamba.*

**Challenging the brief's suggested associations.** "Meaningful time", "slower rhythm" and "nature and community" are what every slow-travel brand claims, so they cannot be the point of difference. "Responsible spending" is believable only once the benefit record is running; before that it is a claim, and the anti-greenwashing rules forbid leading with it.

## 11. The core product: Chamba ke sang, 3 nights

The core is 3 nights at one base with one day out to the other. Each day has one anchor; the rest is choice or nothing at all. It sells in two tiers: **River** (base at H2O House) and **Ridge** (base at Mystic Village), with the same shape mirrored. This is the product the weekend-plus segment and the B2B operators buy.

**River tier, base H2O House:**

| Day | Anchor | Optional (pick one or none) | Deliberately free | Who earns |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Arrive | Pickup at Chamba bus stop; riverside dinner with the host family | — | The evening | Host family, local driver |
| 2 · The valley | Community breakfast with villagers | Chamba town heritage walk plus one craft visit (rumal, chappal or metal art) **or** the H2O plant walk once it is ready | Afternoon by the river; meditation at the ashram if wanted | Breakfast hosts, guide, artisan |
| 3 · The ridge | Day at Mystic Village: road to Rota, 2 km walk in, lunch at Kaku Di Rasoi | One session: Chukh making **or** Gaddi weaving with the SHG | Sunset on the ridge before driving back | Driver, cook, session host, SHG, village guide |
| 4 · Leave | Breakfast; drop to Chamba | — | — | Host family, driver |

**Ridge tier, base Mystic Village:** the same shape reversed. Nights are in a Mystic Village house, day 3 is a river day at H2O House (lunch and plant walk), and it is cheaper per night.

**Design rules built in:**

- **At most one paid session a day.** This protects rest, the segment's main motive (section 4), and stops hosts being rushed through "tasters".
- **One day trip, not a circuit.** It follows the Thrillophilia finding that single-base trips are rising while multi-stop tours fall.
- **Khajjiar meadow is optional, passed on the way.** It is never an anchor, because it is the crowd the hook positions against.
- **Every session is booked and paid as its own line.** That is what makes the Phase 3 benefit record possible.

**Open before sale:**

- Drive times from H2O House to Chamba and to Rota
- Rooms per H2O hut and per Mystic house
- A named guide and artisan in Chamba town
- Session prices set by the hosts themselves

## 12. The seven-day journey: "From the river to the ridge"

This is 6 nights for the learning traveller: 3 at H2O House, then 3 at Mystic Village, with one move in the middle. The arc runs *arrive → listen → learn → make → leave with something you made and someone you know*.

The guest spends at least two sessions with the same teacher, because relationships, not variety, make a long stay worth it. Day 3 and Day 6 afternoons stay unscheduled on purpose. Status codes are those in section 10.

### Day 1 · Arrive at the river

- **Area:** H2O House.
- **Anchor:** pickup at Chamba bus stop; riverside dinner and bonfire with the host family (W).
- **Alternatives:** none. The day exists to slow down.
- **Earners:** host family, driver.
- **Logistics:** most guests come by overnight train to Pathankot (\~10 hrs from Delhi per the listing), then road to Chamba. Expect a tired arrival.
- **Comfort:** private room confirmed in advance.
- **Validate:** H2O House location, rooms, and road time from Chamba.

### Day 2 · The town that made the crafts

- **Area:** Chamba town.
- **Anchor:** a half-day Chamba Rumal session with a GI-registered artisan, the first of two (R, P, C).
- **Alternatives:** chappal or metal-art workshop visit (R, P); the heritage walk (W) as a gentler option.
- **Earners:** artisan, town guide, driver.
- **Value:** learning a GI craft from its maker, the core of the positioning.
- **Time:** about 5 hours including the drive.
- **Food:** lunch at a local eatery in town, not a hotel.
- **Validate:** this anchor rests on a partner NotOnMap does not have yet. Without one, the journey falls back to the heritage walk, and it should not be sold as a learning journey until the partner is confirmed.

### Day 3 · The valley's knowledge

- **Area:** H2O House and the Saal riverbank.
- **Anchor:** the H2O plant walk with a trained local guide, using the transcribed notebook (D, I).
- **Alternatives:** community breakfast with villagers (W); meditation at the ashram (W).
- **Free:** the whole afternoon.
- **Earners:** guide, breakfast hosts.
- **Value:** about 40 plants and their local uses.
- **Comfort:** a moderate walk; route the trail around the "unsafe zone".
- **Validate:** guide training, and whether the trail is finished.

### Day 4 · Up to the ridge

- **Area:** Chamba → Rota → Mystic Village.
- **Anchor:** the 2 km walk in through deodar (W), lunch at Kaku Di Rasoi, and an afternoon village walk with a village guide (W, D).
- **Alternatives:** stop at Auda for the heritage walk on the way (D, C). Optional, and only if Auda's community agrees.
- **Earners:** driver, porter for bags, cook, guide.
- **Evening:** a folk-song evening if the singers want to host one that night (D, C).
- **Comfort:** bags carried up; for low-mobility guests the walk is a blocker. State it at booking.
- **Validate:** porter arrangement; winter snow (Dec–Feb) can close this day.

### Day 5 · The kitchen

- **Area:** Mystic Village.
- **Anchor:** Chamba Chukh making with Anjna Kumari, then cooking Siddu at Kaku Di Rasoi (D, C). Guests eat what they made.
- **Alternatives:** apple orchard time with Lambo Devi in season (D, C).
- **Free:** late afternoon, then sunset on the ridge.
- **Earners:** two cooks, plus produce bought from the village.
- **Value:** a GI-candidate food, and a recipe to take home.

### Day 6 · The loom, and the second session

- **Area:** Mystic Village.
- **Anchor:** half-day Gaddi weaving with Team Udaan SHG; guests continue a small piece over two sessions (D, C).
- **Alternatives:** Dhaulapir camp trek or paragliding for the active (D, I).
- **Evening:** farewell meal cooked with the host family.
- **Earners:** SHG and the named teacher, adventure guides.
- **Give back:** join Team Parivartan's regular plastic-free walk only if they want guests along, and pay their fund a fixed fee. Guests' labour is not counted as "impact".
- **Validate:** adventure licensing and insurance.

### Day 7 · Leave with something

- **Area:** Mystic Village → Rota → Pathankot or Chamba.
- **Anchor:** sunrise on the ridge; walk out; drop.
- **Earners:** porter, driver.
- **Take-away:** the piece they made, the recipe, and the benefit record (Phase 3).

**Seasonal variants:**

- **Minjar departure** (late July–early August): Day 2 moves to the fair, but monsoon roads need buffer time.
- **Apple season** at Mystic Village.
- **Winter** (Dec–Feb): reverse the bases or stay at the river only. Snow and access can cut the ridge.

**Fair pricing method:**

1. Ask each provider what a half-day must pay to be worth more than their alternative day's work. That is their floor.
2. NotOnMap adds its fee openly on top.
3. Rotation happens within a price band, so no host is pushed to undercut another.

This directly answers the "village stays should be cheap" pressure that Himalayan Ark's founder describes (section 3).

## 15. Operating model

A pure marketplace would need volume and technology NotOnMap doesn't have. A full community network would need institutions that exist only in Mystic Village today.

## 16. Business model and price build-up

Build the price bottom-up from what providers ask for, then add each other cost as a visible line. NotOnMap earns from four streams. None of them should require cutting a provider's price. All prices below are placeholders until the field round collects host floors.

**Price build-up for one journey.** The same structure works for 3 nights or 6.

| Line | How it is set | Who receives it | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stay | Host's floor per night | Host family | Collect in field |
| Meals | Cook's floor per meal | Cook or host | Collect in field |
| Sessions (craft, cooking, guiding) | Provider's floor per half-day | The named provider or SHG | Collect in field |
| Transport and porters | Fare card agreed with drivers | Drivers, porters | Collect in field |
| Village coordinator | Fixed per guest-day | Coordinator | NotOnMap to set |
| Village fund | A % of all local provider lines, agreed by the village | Village fund account | Village to decide; benchmarks use 5–20% (section 3) |
| NotOnMap fee | A % on top, shown as one line | NotOnMap | Set after the cost model below |
| Payment processing | Actual gateway cost | Payment provider | Known after setup |
| GST | By rule | Government | Accommodation priced at ₹7,500 or less per unit per day is [taxed at 5% without input tax credit](https://taxguru.in/?p=1031968) since September 2025. Get a CA to confirm treatment for bundled packages |

**NotOnMap's revenue streams:**

1. **Trip fee** on direct (B2C) bookings.
2. **Net-rate margin on B2B sales.** Operators get a net rate and add their own markup; their markup is not local spend and the benefit record says so.
3. **Institutional and educational programmes.** Higher-value groups in the off-season.
4. **Grant or CSR funding for readiness upgrades**: bathrooms, registration fees, training. This is kept outside the guest price, so guests don't pay for capital works twice.

Commission on a guest's own craft purchases applies only where the artisan has agreed in writing.

**Cost heads to model before setting the fee:** coordinator pay; NotOnMap staff time per booking; marketing and B2B sales; payment fees; insurance; training; the annual verification review; Home Stay registration support; technology.

The fee should cover these at pilot volume, plus a target surplus, and be published as a single percentage.

**One rule above all:** discounts, promotions and points are funded from NotOnMap's fee or a sponsor, never by reducing a provider's line.

## 17. The Chamba Local Spend Record

The idea holds up, but only if it reports *payments*, not *benefit*. It also has to take the guest's full price as the denominator and count only what a bank or UPI reference proves. Built that way, it goes further than the Ripple Score (section 3), and it can be run by a small team with providers who use only a phone.

**The name.** I recommend **Chamba Local Spend Record**, not any of the four suggested:

- "Benefit" and "Impact" claim an outcome that a payment does not prove.
- "Certificate" implies an outside audit.
- "Passport" invites gamification.
- "Record" says exactly what it is.

### Metrics

| Metric | Numerator | Denominator | Excluded | Evidence | Frequency | Limitation |

| **Local Value Retention (LVR)** | DLPR numerator plus verified payments to locally owned businesses in Chamba district (e.g. a guesthouse whose ownership is not yet confirmed as local household) | Same | NotOnMap's fee (shown separately); GST; gateway; non-local purchases | As above, plus ownership declaration | Per journey | Ownership is self-declared; providers' onward spending is not traced |
| **Providers paid** | Count of distinct verified payees | — | Payees under ₹100 | Ledger | Per journey; monthly | A count, not a depth measure |
| **Distribution by category** | Verified payments per category (stay, food, sessions, guiding, transport, coordination, village fund) | Total verified local payments | — | Ledger | Monthly | — |
| **Inclusion** | Payments to women-led providers, SHGs and under-30s | Total verified local payments | Anyone who hasn't consented to be counted | Self-declaration with consent | Quarterly; aggregate only | Self-declared; never shown per person |

### Payment models compared

| Model | Guest experience | Proof | Provider cash flow | Verdict |

### Verification

Evidence is graded, and only grade A counts in the headline figures.

| Grade | Evidence | Counted as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Bank or UPI transfer reference to an account verified in the provider's own name | **Verified** |
| B | Provider confirms receipt by WhatsApp or phone reply | Confirmed, shown separately |
| C | Cash with a photographed receipt | Reported, not verified |

**Controls against manipulation:**

- **One account per provider,** checked once (a penny test or a passbook photo). Never a NotOnMap staff member's or coordinator's account.
- **The coordinator can't be the payee** for another provider's line.
- **Duplicates blocked:** every line carries a booking ID, and the ledger rejects a second payment against the same ID.
- **Call-backs:** 10% of payments each month are checked by phone by someone outside the booking team, asking "did you receive ₹X on this date, and did you have to give any of it back?"
- **A grievance number** that goes to the village committee and NotOnMap's director, not to the coordinator.
- **An annual review** of the ledger against bank statements by an outside party, such as a CA or a university team. The review's date and scope are printed on every record.

### What the guest receives

A one-page Record by email and WhatsApp within 7 days of checkout. It contains:

- Journey ID, dates and places
- Total paid
- DLPR and LVR as percentages and rupees
- Number of providers paid, by category
- What was excluded and why
- How the figures were calculated
- The date of the last outside review
- A QR code to a public verification page

The verification page shows the same totals, the ledger's reference count, and a correction log. It never shows names, phone numbers or individual earnings unless a provider has explicitly consented to appear ("Taught by Anjna Kumari") without an amount.

### Anti-greenwashing policy

India's CCPA [Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Greenwashing or Misleading Environmental Claims, 2024](https://kpmg.com/in/en/insights/2024/12/firstnotes-guidelines-to-regulate-greenwashing-and-misleading-environmental-claims.html) already require environmental claims to be substantiated. Chamba ke sang applies the same discipline to its social claims:

1. Only verified payments in headline numbers; confirmed and reported figures shown separately and labelled.
2. No "100%", "community-owned", "carbon-neutral" or "zero-waste" claims unless evidenced and dated.
3. No multipliers presented as cash; no counting guest labour or unpaid hosting as benefit.
4. NotOnMap's fee, GST, gateway costs and operator markups always shown.
5. Payment is never described as "improved livelihoods". That claim needs the annual provider survey.
6. Every figure carries its date, method and last review date.
7. A public correction log: errors are fixed and shown, not quietly edited.

### Points and rewards: my verdict is no points

A points scheme rewards guests for *choosing* local options. It invites providers to compete on price to attract point-hungry guests, and it makes families who need a private bathroom feel penalised for a legitimate choice.

Instead:

- The Record itself is the recognition.
- One sponsor-funded thank-you after a second trip (for example a free session), paid at the provider's full rate by the sponsor.
- An optional, opt-in public tally of "rupees paid to Chamba providers by guests this season". It is collective, not a personal leaderboard.

### Worked example (hypothetical numbers, not estimates)

Two guests, the 6-night journey, a total of **₹50,000 paid**.

| Line | ₹ | Payee | Evidence | Counts in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H2O House, 3 nights | 16,500 | Property owner | UPI reference | LVR only, until household ownership is confirmed |
| Mystic Village house, 3 nights | 6,000 | Host family | UPI reference | DLPR |
| Meals (cook A) | 2,000 | Cook | UPI reference | DLPR |
| Meals (cook B) | 1,000 | Cook | Cash, receipt photo | Reported only |
| Sessions: rumal ×2, Chukh, Siddu, weaving ×2 (SHG plus teacher), plant walk, village walk | 7,500 | 7 payees | UPI references | DLPR |
| Transport and porter | 4,500 | 2 drivers, 1 porter | UPI references | DLPR |
| Village coordinator | 1,500 | Coordinator | UPI reference | DLPR |
| Village fund (5% of provider lines) | 1,875 | Fund account | Bank reference | DLPR |
| NotOnMap fee | 7,000 | NotOnMap | — | Shown separately |
| GST (5% on accommodation) | 1,125 | Government | — | Shown separately |
| Payment processing | 1,000 | Gateway | — | Shown separately |
| **Total** | **50,000** |  |  |  |

From this:

- **Verified direct local payments** come to ₹23,375, so DLPR = 23,375 ÷ 50,000 = **46.8%**, paid to 14 verified payees (12 individuals, 1 SHG, 1 village fund).
- **LVR** = (23,375 + 16,500) ÷ 50,000 = **79.8%**.
- **Reported but not verified:** ₹1,000 cash.

The example is deliberately unflattering. The biggest single line, the premium base, doesn't count as community payment until its ownership is shown. That is the honesty that makes the other numbers credible.

**Discrepancy handling.** Say cook B reports receiving ₹800. The line is marked "disputed" on the verification page, the coordinator and an outside caller check within 7 days, and the resolution is logged. If money is missing, NotOnMap pays the difference and the Record is reissued.

## 18. Governance, consent and readiness

The community decides what is offered, how often, at what price and what is never sold. NotOnMap decides what meets the guest standard. Both rules are written down before the first pilot guest arrives.

**Who speaks for the community.** No single person or body does. In Mystic Village, decisions go to a joint meeting of:

- the Village Development Committee
- Team Udaan SHG
- the youth (Team Parivartan)
- at least two households that don't host

Meetings are run separately for women, youth and elders first. NotOnMap already used this format in the Survey Report (§XIV). H2O House is a private family property, so it is governed by a provider agreement, not a village vote. Auda and Saal Valley join only after their own meetings.

**What local people control:**

| Decision | Decided by | How often |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which experiences are offered | Providers individually, with the committee's agreement | Before listing; reviewed each season |
| What is never commercialised | The village meeting | Before the pilot; any time after |
| Guest caps per house and per month | The village meeting | Each season |
| Provider prices | Each provider sets their own floor | Each season |
| Use of the village fund | The village meeting, with the account visible to all | Quarterly |
| Complaints about guests or NotOnMap | The village committee, with escalation to NotOnMap's director | As raised |

**Candidates for the "not for sale" list,** for the community to decide, not NotOnMap:

- Rituals during the Manimahesh Yatra (Nahoun and Parvi) and other worship
- The Toli Mata shrine at Auda
- Household kitchens on festival days
- Photography of children
- Any livelihood activity done in a way that would put animals under stress for guests

The Inception Report lists "rabbit photography" as a planned experience. I recommend dropping it unless an animal-welfare standard is agreed.

**Informed consent.** Consent is recorded per person, in Hindi or the local dialect, by written or voice note. It covers four separate permissions:

1. Hosting or teaching
2. Photos and video
3. Name on listings
4. Name on Local Spend Records

It is renewed every year and can be withdrawn at any time without penalty. Minors are never featured, and the 57 existing profiles need this consent before reuse.

**Cultural and intellectual property.** Recipes, designs and songs belong to their makers.

- No recording of songs or sessions for commercial use without separate consent and payment.
- GI crafts are taught only by authorised producers.
- Guest-made pieces are personal, not for resale.

**Visitor code**, given before arrival and signed at check-in:

- Ask before every photo.
- Follow each house's rules on alcohol and dress.
- Carry waste out.
- No cash or sweets for children.
- No entering shrines or kitchens unless invited.

**Grievance.** There are two channels: one for guests, one for providers and any resident, including people who don't host. Each gets acknowledgement in 48 hours, resolution or escalation in 14 days, and a log reviewed at each quarterly meeting.

**Decline, modify, discontinue.** Any provider can pause an experience by telling the coordinator, with no reason needed and no penalty. The village meeting can close any experience. NotOnMap removes paused items from sale within 24 hours.

### Readiness tiers

A provider moves up over time rather than being shut out. Only Tier 2 and above are sold to direct guests; Tier 1 is used for pilots with informed guests.

| Area | Tier 0 · Not yet ready | Tier 1 · Ready with support | Tier 2 · Verified and operational | Tier 3 · Specialist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Willingness and consent | Unclear | Consent recorded | Consent recorded; has hosted at least 3 guests | Trains others |
| Registration (HP Home Stay Rules 2025) | Not registered | Application filed with NotOnMap's help | Registered; certificate on file | Registered |
| Toilet and water | Shared or none | Shared, clean, with running water | Private bathroom or clearly disclosed shared; safe drinking water (filtered) | Same |
| Hygiene and food safety | No check | Basic training done | Passes the NotOnMap checklist twice in a row | Food-safety trained |
| Fire and electrical | No equipment | Plan to meet the 2025 rules | Equipment per the 2025 rules; safe wiring checked | Same |
| Emergency | No plan | Coordinator's phone and nearest vehicle known | Written plan with hospital distance and an evacuation route; first-aid kit | First-aid trained host |
| Activity safety (treks, paragliding) | Not assessed | — | Licences and insurance verified | Certified instructors |
| Accessibility | Not disclosed | Disclosed honestly | Disclosed, with photos | Adapted for low mobility |
| Waste | Open dumping | Segregation started | Segregation; plastic carried out with Team Parivartan | Leads village practice |

**What NotOnMap provides:** training, registration help, grant-funded bathroom and safety upgrades, the checklist visits.

**What providers are responsible for:** daily cleanliness, honest listings, keeping agreed prices and keeping guests safe in their home.

Phase 3 checked the 2025 rules only through secondary sources (section 5). Confirm each Tier 2 requirement against the gazette before it is used to exclude anyone.

## 21. Red team

The concept survives scrutiny, but three weaknesses are serious.

1. **Most guests will not travel to Chamba *for* this.** They will add it to a Dalhousie trip, so the 3-night core and the B2B channel carry the business, and the 7-day journey is a niche.
2. **The model depends on partners and data that don't exist yet:** an artisan, price floors and bank accounts.
3. **It cannot run without NotOnMap coordination for at least two seasons.** It needs grant support for fixed costs until then.

### The founder's hard questions

| Question | Honest answer |
| --- | --- |
| Why travel specifically to Chamba for this? | Most won't. Learning travellers might, for GI crafts with named teachers. Everyone else adds it to a trip already heading to Dalhousie |
| How is it different from an ordinary homestay holiday? | Named teachers, real sessions, at most one session a day, and a verified Record. If the sessions don't happen reliably, it isn't different |
| Do residents genuinely want visitors? | Mystic Village shows interest; Auda's evidence conflicts; non-hosting households haven't been asked. Phase 1 asks them |
| What if they don't want something commercialised? | The "not for sale" list is theirs and binding (section 18) |
| Are we creating unpaid work? | Yes, unless we pay the person who does it. In host families that is often women cooking and cleaning while payment goes to a man's account. Pay by person, by line |
| How do we know the money reaches people? | Transfer references to accounts in their own name, call-backs and an outside review. We still can't see how money is shared inside a household, and we say so |
| How do we prevent false claims? | The anti-greenwashing policy, graded evidence and the correction log (section 17) |
| What if providers disagree with our standards? | The checklist is co-written in Phase 1, tiers let people improve, and anyone can decline to list |
| Off-season? | December–February: the river base only, institutional groups, craft and Chukh sales. Accept lower income; don't promise year-round work |
| What if demand is low? | Costs are mostly per guest, so low demand hurts NotOnMap's fixed costs more than providers. The B2B channel is the main demand lever |
| What if demand exceeds capacity? | Caps hold; a waitlist; expand only where a village asks |
| Can it run without heavy NGO coordination? | Not for two seasons. Coordinators take over day-to-day by season 3 |
| How does it survive financially? | The fee covers per-trip costs; fixed costs need grant or CSR money through the pilot. A plan that claims otherwise is optimistic |
| What stops copying? | Itineraries are easy to copy. Consented relationships, the provider ledger and a credible Record are not |
| Are we deepening tourism dependency? | Cap tourism's share of household time and income; grow product sales and hotel supply alongside |
| What if guests want luxury? | Don't sell to them. H2O House is the ceiling, and the copy says so |
| Is seven days justified? | Only for learning travellers and slow walkers. The core is 3 nights |
| Biggest risk to community trust? | A few households capturing most guests, or one late payment. Hence the spread KPI and the 3-day payout rule |

### Five assumptions to validate before launch

1. **Host price floors produce a guest price the two segments will pay.** Test with 10 operators and 10 past guests.
2. **At least one GI rumal artisan will teach on a regular schedule** at their own price.
3. **Providers have, or will open, bank accounts in their own names** and accept digital payouts.
4. **The village agrees caps and rotation, and hosts don't break away** to take guests independently (the Himalayan Ark experience).
5. **H2O House is a local household enterprise** with enough rooms for the River tier, and its ownership can be disclosed on the Record.

## 22. Digital product direction

Don't build a platform yet. The pilot needs four plain tools, all of which NotOnMap can run without developers:

1. One catalogue sheet
2. One ledger sheet
3. WhatsApp for providers
4. A Record template

The teammates' Journey Builder prototype is a strong front end for this, but it has to read from the catalogue's status field so it never offers what isn't ready.

**Pilot stack (now):**

- **Catalogue:** one row per experience, with its readiness tier, consent status, price floor and season. The Journey Builder reads it, and its rule that "the logic never looks at an experience's name" makes this easy. Only Tier 2+ items with consent are shown to paying guests.
- **Ledger:** one row per payable line, with booking ID, payee, amount, transfer reference, date and evidence grade.
- **Providers:** WhatsApp messages and voice notes for bookings and confirmations, because no provider should need an app. The reply "received" becomes grade B evidence.
- **Payments:** a gateway for the guest; UPI or bank transfers to providers, with split settlement later.
- **Record:** generated from the ledger into a one-page PDF, plus a verification page per journey ID.

**Fix in the Journey Builder before it goes public:**

- Rename it to Chamba ke sang.
- Hide anything below Tier 2 or without consent.
- Show the price build-up lines (section 16).
- Add the visitor code and the Record explanation.
- Replace placeholder travel times with field-measured ones.

| User | Core journeys | Modules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Traveller | Choose by interest and pace → see who teaches and what each line pays → book → receive the Record → verify it | Journey Builder, booking, Record and verification |

## Founder summary

**Chamba ke sang turns Chamba's pass-through visitors into guests who stay, learn from named local people, and receive a verified record of what their stay paid those people.** It starts small: two bases, one village, one season, three gates.

**The problem.** Chamba district had about 11.9 lakh tourist arrivals in 2024, yet NotOnMap's own study found that Dalhousie hotels keep the flow and send guests to Chamba for the day. Weekend traffic on the Khajjiar–Dalhousie road jams for hours. Village households earn little and unpredictably from this flow.

**The opportunity.** Indian travellers are shifting to slower, single-base trips. Most travellers globally say they want their money to stay local. Chamba has things no other valley has: GI-registered crafts (rumal, chappal, metal art), a living Gaddi village already organised for guests, and 57 people with skills worth learning.

**The solution.** A 3-night core (River or Ridge tier) for weekend-plus guests, sold mainly through B2B operators, plus a 6-night "river to ridge" journey for learning travellers. It is built on one grid of anchors, optional pathways, free time and invited encounters.

**What makes it distinctive.** Named teachers, not attractions. At most one session a day. The Chamba Local Spend Record, which counts only verified payments against the full price and publishes its corrections. No regional peer does this, and even the Ripple Score stops short of it.

**How communities benefit.**

- Each provider sets their own floor price and is paid into their own account within 3 days.
- A village fund takes an agreed share.
- The village decides caps, rotation and what is never sold.
- Women's and youth income is tracked, with consent.

**How impact is measured.** DLPR and LVR from transfer references, call-backs and an annual outside review. The worked example shows 46.8% verified direct local payment on a hypothetical ₹50,000 trip, and why an honest number beats a flattering one.

**How the pilot works.** Discovery runs December–January, build February–March, and capped paid journeys April–June 2027. Each gate must be passed: consent and an artisan; then a tested ledger and Tier 1 providers; then 80% of providers wanting to continue.

**Decisions and support needed from the founder:**

- [ ] Approve the hybrid model, with NotOnMap as merchant of record and paid village coordinators
- [ ] Name one pilot owner inside NotOnMap
- [ ] Approve the "payment, not benefit" rule and the anti-greenwashing policy, including publishing NotOnMap's fee
- [ ] Confirm H2O House facts and whether its ownership can be shown on the Record
- [ ] Open introductions to Chamba rumal and metal-art artisans
- [ ] Seek grant or CSR funding for fixed pilot costs (coordinators, outside review, registration and bathroom upgrades)
- [ ] Retire "Chamba Impact Expedition" and drop visitor teaching at the school
